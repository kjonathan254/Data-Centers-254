## 1. BACKGROUND

The East African Community (EAC) is a regional organization mandated by the
Governments of the Republic of Burundi, Democratic Republic of Congo
(DRC), Republic of Kenya, Republic of Rwanda, Republic of South Sudan,
Federal Republic of Somalia, the Republic of Uganda and the United Republic
of Tanzania (URT) to spearhead the East African economic, social and political
integration agenda. The key building blocks towards economic, social and
political integration of the region as articulated in the Treaty for the
Establishment of the East African Community include the Customs Union,
Common Market, Monetary Union and ultimately the Political Federation. The
EAC entered a fully-fledged Customs Union on 1st January 2010, while the EAC
Common Market was adopted in July 2010. The Protocol for the establishment
of the East African Monetary Union (EAMU) was signed in November 2013 and
its implementation is underway with establishment of the institutions for the

BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE
East Africa region’s data ecosystem is undergoing rapid transformation driven
by increasingly complex digital infrastructure, rapidly technological
advancement and the growing need for data to inform decision-making. This
dynamic environment offers an opportunity to facilitate collaboration and
streamline data sharing for enhanced evidence-informed decision making
(EIDM). However, it also presents challenges regarding the
effective management, storage and secure sharing of data while
safeguarding data subjects against data privacy breaches.

The EAC has acknowledged the need for developing a harmonized regional
legal framework on data protection and privacy; and a Mechanism for Cross-
border Data Flows for the Community to facilitate a common approach
towards the ethical and responsible processing, management and sharing of
data throughout the data value chain in the region. An EAC Mechanism for
Cross-border Data Flows aims to provide a reference for EAC Members’s
regulations on personal data, including delineate access controls, permissions,
and data sharing policies, striking a balance between data accessibility for
authorized users and safeguarding against data privacy breaches across the
Partner States. The EAC harmonization Mechanism for Cross-border Data Flows
shall reflect best practices and regional- and internationally-agreed principles
for data flows across the borders of EAC Partner States, including those
reflected in the AU Malabo Convention, OECD Privacy Framework, Convenion
108+, the APEC Privacy Framework, and the EU’s GDPR.

In addition, the EAC Members have agreed to implement a regional
mechanism to facilitate data sharing under the umbrella of the Eastern African

Community. The goal of an EAC Mechanism for Cross-border Data Flows (“the
Mechanism”) is to provide a channel for lawful and expedient data flows even
in the absence of harmonized regulations or effective data protection and
privacy institutions. The Mechanism shall allow for data to be seamlessly shared
within the region while ensuring robust safeguards for the treatment of personal
data. This study will focus on EAC Partners States. In parallel, the IGAD
Secretariat is commissioning a similar procurement also under EARDIP covering
IGAD members. Given the shared membership between these two
organizations, it is expected a close cooperation between both initiatives.

## 2. OBJECTIVE OF THE ASSIGMENT

The main objective is to develop a legal and policy framework to harmonize
legislation for data protection and privacy and to advance toward the
implementation of the mechanism for sharing data across border within the
EAC region and with international partners.

## 3. SCOPE OF ASSIGNMENT

The Consultant will conduct an in-depth review of the EAC countries’ current
legal, policy and institutional framework for data protection and privacy, with
a view to providing practical guidance for the EAC Member States to
advance in the harmonization of data protection and privacy legislation and
regulations and the establishment of an EAC Mechanism for Cross-border Data
Flows.

The specific tasks include:

### 3.1 STOCKTAKING REPORT ON DATA PROTECTION AND PRIVACY IN THE EAC

AND PRINCIPLES FOR HARMONIZATION. The report shall build on existing
studies, such as the Comparative Study of Data Protection Legislation in EAC
by Allan Mukuku and Alex Assenga (2024), to offer an in-depth review of
current legal, institutional, and administrative framework governing the sharing
of (privately generated) personal and non-personal data in EAC with a view

a) Review and analyze existing data protection and privacy policies and

Partner States and their internation al partners.

b) To review and analyze existing data protection and privacy policies and

e) To conduct consultations with key public, private, academia and civil
society stakeholders on data protection and privacy policies and legal
frameworks including those on cross-border data flows.

f) To propose draft principles for the East African Community on data
protection and privacy Framework including those on cross-border data
flows, taking into account the regional, continental and international
legal and policy frameworks on data protection and privacy.

BORDER DATA FLOWS. Building in existing literature and leveraging the research
and cosultations conducted under activity 3.1, the Consultant will
h) Propose recommendations for the establishment of the EAC Mechanism
for Cross-border Data Flows. In light of the current disparities and the
absence of particular, the recommendations may consider different
scenarios, such as ) expanding the use of existing channels for data
sharing, such as standards contractual clauses, or ii) adopting a new
mechanism for the EAC, taking from different cross-border data sharing
mechanisms around the world, including GDPR, APEC CBPR, to
maximize uptake in the EAC region and compatibility with international
partners
i) Identify and plan out key actions needed to implement in the
Mechanism. The road map should elaborate on the legal instruments,
actors (public or private), roles and procedures that the Mechanism

mechanism; ii) provisional solutions for those countries that lack an
effective data protection and privacy agency; iii) how to certify
compliance by individual stakeholders, etc.. To this end, the Road Map
will:
i. Identify responsible entities for the implementation of these
recommendations, bearing in mind those who already are or
those who need to be empowered, resourced and skilled to
deliver this.

ii. Elaborate specific, actionable activities that different actors in the
region (regional and national; public and private) must undertake
to implement those recommendations.

## 6. COORDINATION WITH RELATED ACTIVITIES

Various countries in the sub-region, such as the Republic of Kenya, the
Republic of South Sudan and the Republic of Uganda, are members of both
EAC and IGAD regional organizations. Under EARDIP, the Secretariat of the
EAC is also commissioning similar activities to the ones described in this
document, in particular regarding the proposal for principles for regulatory
harmonization.

Throughout the duration of the project, the Consultant is expected to
coordinate closely with IGAD and World Bank authorities, as well as other
consultants team carrying out similar tasks for the EAC. It is expected that
deliverables under this procurement are aligned and fully consistent with similar
deliverables being procured by the EAC.

The Consultants are expected to coordinate on substance, in particular
regarding the proposed principles for regulatory harmonization, as well as on
activities that can be conducted jointly, especially consultations with
government officials in countries that are members of both the EAC and IGAD.

While Consultants are not expected to provide identical products, the
Consultants to both IGAD and EAC procurement shall strive to propose
recommendations and guidance that are consistent or mutually compatible.
