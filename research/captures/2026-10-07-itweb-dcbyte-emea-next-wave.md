## Figures in the article (all attributable to DC Byte analysis + webinar)

- "The EMEA region has 93GW of IT capacity, spanning live facilities, construction, committed projects and early-stage developments."
- "Almost a quarter of this is concentrated in Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Paris and Dublin, collectively known as FLAP-D" / "the FLAP-D markets alone account for 24% of EMEA's total capacity"
- Regional split: "EMEA's 93GW of total data centre capacity is heavily concentrated in Europe, which accounts for 88% of the region's capacity... the Middle East accounts for 9%, while Africa represents just 3% of EMEA's total data centre capacity"
- "Africa represents just 3% of EMEA capacity, leaving significant room for expansion. (Source: DC Byte)"
- Next-wave markets named by DC Byte lead analyst Christian Tshishiku: "In the Middle East, we have Abu Dhabi and Riyadh as well. And then, in Africa, we are talking about South Africa as a whole."
- Constraint framing: "power and land constraints are pushing developers and customers to consider alternative locations"; AI workloads "can require large amounts of power concentrated in individual facilities or campuses"
- Land advantage: "If we look in EMEA, Africa and the Middle East probably have a better advantage on that because they have more available space in comparison to Europe, where they have built infrastructure across the whole geography."
- Caveat quoted: land availability "does not mean every site will be suitable for a data centre. Operators still have to secure sufficient power and assess how quickly that power can be delivered."
