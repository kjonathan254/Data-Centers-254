## Key verbatim extracts (DC-relevant)

### Law 48/2018 - Article 30 (EIA obligation + delegation)

- "Article 30: Projects that must undergo an environmental impact assessment and its procedure"
- "The list of projects that must undergo an environmental impact assessment before they obtain authorization for their implementation is established by an Order of the Minister. An Order of the Minister also issues instructions and procedures for conducting environmental impact assessment."

### Law 48/2018 - Articles 31-34 (adjacent machinery)

- Art. 31: "Every policy, strategy, plan and programme must undergo a strategic environmental assessment." (procedures by Order of the Minister)
- Art. 32: "Every project that may have significant impact on the environment must undergo an environmental audit during and after its implementation." (list of audit projects also by Order of the Minister)
- Art. 33: "The environmental impact assessment, environmental audit and strategic environmental assessment must be approved by the Authority or another State organ authorised in writing to do so by the Authority." (Authority = REMA)
- Art. 34: "The consultancy cost for environmental audit and environmental impact assessment are borne by the project initiator."

### Ministerial Order 001/2019 - structure

- Article 3: full EIA list = Annex I of the Order. Article 4: partial ESIA (PESIA) list = Annex II. Article 5 (paraphrase of body text): projects on neither Annex I nor Annex II "are not subject" to EIA, BUT a project "not listed ... [with] similar [nature]" to a listed one can be caught by analogy; the Authority decides borderline cases (verbatim body at order p.1-2: "1° the list of projects that must undergo an [EIA] ... 2° full environmental impact assessment: ... 3° partial environmental impact [assessment]: ...").

### Order 001/2019 - Annex I (FULL EIA list), the DC-relevant items

- "1° all buildings classified as residential, commercial, administrative or institutional sports facilities, social, cultural, and assembly and religious buildings, hotels, health facilities, educational buildings, or other publicly accessible facilities fulfilling at least two of the following conditions: a. having capacity to host more than five hundred (500) people; b. having a total floor area exceeding one thousand and five hundred square meters (1500 sqm); c. built in plot size exceeding one thousand square meters (1000 sqm)."
- "2° industries;"
- "12° construction of hydro-dams, hydropower plants and electrical lines of high and medium voltage;"

### Order 001/2019 - Annex II (PARTIAL ESIA / PESIA list), DC-adjacent items

- "1° towers and antennas;"
- "2° all types of buildings, except those qualified for full environmental impact assessment fulfilling at least two of the following conditions: a. with a capacity to host more than two hundred (200) people but not exceeding five hundred (500) people; b. with a total floor area exceeding one thousand and five hundred square meters (1500 sqm); c. built in a plot size exceeding one thousand square meters (1000 sqm)."
- "3° micro hydroelectric power plants."

- GAP SERVED: Rwanda "environmental" pillar gap - "No claims researched yet (REMA EIA requirements for large facilities)". This capture delivers the complete statutory chain: framework law (Art. 30-34) -> the operative trigger list (Order 001/2019 Annex I/II).
- DC READ-ACROSS (analyst inference, not statute text): a data-centre campus is not named anywhere in the Order. Its most likely statutory hook is Annex I item 1° (buildings "classified as commercial/administrative" - publicly-accessible phrasing is awkward for DCs but DCs fit neither list cleanly) via the floor-area (>1500 sqm) + plot-size (>1000 sqm) conditions, which a mid/large DC campus typically meets BOTH of; item 2° "industries" is generic and unqualified; item 12° catches the electrical line infrastructure, not the facility. Because item 1° is conditioned on building classification ("publicly accessible facilities"), whether a DC is caught is a classification question under the Order + building-code instruments - the capture records the trigger text WITHOUT resolving the classification question editorially. This is exactly the nuance a future claim would have to carry.
- SAFEGUARD: do NOT shorten "fulfilling at least two of the following conditions" to "any large building requires EIA" - the two-of-three structure and the building-classification premise are the claim-critical details.
- FOUR-BASELINE DISCIPLINE: no market/market-size figures involved; the sqm/people thresholds are regulatory triggers. Never blend with facility-count or MW baselines.
