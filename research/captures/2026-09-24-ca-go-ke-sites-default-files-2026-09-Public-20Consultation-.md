## Executive Summary

This consultation document proposes a standalone licence category for co-location data
centre operators rather than placing co-location data centres under the Network Facilities
Provider -Tier 2 licence category. This proposal recognises that co-location data centres
provide hosting, colocation, power, cooling, storage and computing infrastructure, and do
not provide telecommunications services directly to end users. A separate licence category
would give the Authority appropriate regulatory visibility over critical digital infrastructure
while avoiding the over-application of Network Facilities Provider (NFP) licensing
requirements. Stakeholders and the public are invited to provide inputs and comments on
the proposal.

BACKGROUND
1. The Communications Authority of Kenya (the Authority) was established in 1999
under the Kenya Information and Communications Act, 1998. The Authority regulates
Kenya’s Information and Communications Technology (ICT) sector, including
broadcasting, cybersecurity, multimedia, telecommunications, electronic commerce (e-
commerce), and postal and courier services. It promotes the development of these
subsectors through licensing, competition management, frequency and numbering
resource management, and ICT consumer protection.

2. The Authority licenses telecommunications systems and services in accordance with
section 24(1) of the Kenya Information and Communications Act, 1998, which
provides that: “No person shall operate a telecommunication system or provide any
telecommunication services except in accordance with a valid licence granted under
this Act.”

3. The ICT Sector Policy Guidelines, 2020, direct government agencies to promote,
encourage and license private sector investment in carrier neutral data centres by
companies incorporated for that purpose.

4. The Authority’s 2023–2027 Strategic Plan identifies the promotion of meaningful
connectivity to ICT services as a key result area. This objective includes ensuring
efficient ICT markets and fostering competition through targeted regulatory
interventions. The proposed standalone licence category for data centres is therefore
aligned with the attainment of this strategic objective.

5. The Authority has established a telecommunications market structure and application
requirements, which are instruments that provide market access to the regulated
telecommunication industry in Kenya.

6. The current telecommunications market structure, which was revised in April 2026, is
based on technology and service neutrality principles. This approach was considered
critical towards simplifying and facilitating market entry by minimizing regulatory
requirements and processes for evolving and dynamic technologies.

7. Under the current telecommunications market structure, co-location data centre
operators are licensed under the Network Facilities Provider (NFP) – Tier 2 (NFP-T2)
licence category. Whereas this categorisation was designed to give the Authority
visibility over data centre operations, it has also placed data centres within a licensing
class designed primarily for entities that establish and operate telecommunications
network facilities across the country.

A. Co-location Data Centre Operators
A.1. Need for Licensing
8. The National ICT policy guidelines, 2020, promote infrastructure sharing as a principle
of prudent management of capital-intensive projects, noting that Data Centres will be
deployed nationally and regionally. The policy requires the Authority to license and
oversee such operators, including Data Centres. It also specifically states: “The
government will promote, encourage and license private sector investment in neutral
data centres by companies incorporated for that purpose”.

A.2. Current Status
9. Data centres are critical to the digital economy since they host systems that enable
internet access, financial services, healthcare, e-government, agriculture, and education
platforms that Kenya has identified as central to its Vision 2030 and Digital Economy
Blueprint commitments.

10. Co-location Data Centres Operators are currently licensed under the Network Facilities
Provider (NFP) – Tier 2 (NFP-T2) licence category.

A.3. Considerations
11. Data centres provide co-location, power, and cooling services, and do not transmit
signals, assign or use spectrum, or provide subscriber-facing services.

12. The proposed change from NFP level to a standalone licensing category for co-location
data centre operations will enhance the country’s competitive edge compared to
regional peers, hence attract investments.

13. The proposed licensing framework will enable the required Regulatory oversight over
co-location data centre operations that host critical facilities/infrastructure for entities
who provide ICT services licensed by the Authority.

A.4. Proposals
14. The proposed framework is a standalone Data Centre licence category as data centres
host computational power, storage, and applications necessary to support an enterprise
business, and do not transmit signals, assign or use spectrum, or provide subscriber-
facing services.

15. The proposed licensing framework will enable the required regulatory visibility on the
operations of colocation data centres in Kenya that host systems that are critical in the
development of the digital economy in the country, including facilities for entities who
provide services that are licensed by the Authority.

## 17. Entities that have Network Facility Provider (NFP) or Application Service Provider

(ASP) licences shall be permitted to establish and operate data centres without the need
for a data centre licence.

A.5. USF fees
18. USF fees will be handled as provided for in the law (section 84 J (3) KICA Cap 411A).

Currently, USF fee is charged at the rate of 0.5% of the Annual Gross Revenue with
respect to licensed services.
