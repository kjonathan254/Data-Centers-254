Kenya has the steam. So where are the AI data centres?

Olkaria has powered Kenya for four decades. In May 2024, Microsoft and G42 announced a $1 billion digital ecosystem investment with a geothermal-powered data centre campus at its centre. The announced first phase was 100 MW. Kenya's entire verified live IT load today: 10.5 MW.

Two years on, the campus is still an announcement. Reporting in 2026 points to two obstacles: a power requirement that grew toward 1,000 MW, about a third of the country's installed capacity, and a dispute over guaranteed capacity payments that stalled the talks. The government position is that the project is "not failed or withdrawn".

The bigger lesson: renewable energy is an advantage, and it is not the entire equation. Power procurement, grid capacity, transmission and bankable contracts all decide whether announcements become live load.

Our latest analysis examines what happened at Olkaria, what Fervo's geothermal project in Utah teaches about commercial structure, and what would move Kenya from announcements to live capacity. While you read, note the 77 MW already under construction in Nairobi. That build is the market's quiet answer.
