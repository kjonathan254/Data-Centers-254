## 1. LINKEDIN (flagship, 200-250 words)

Kenya's digital infrastructure is bigger than the headlines. But smaller than the announcements.

Here is what the DC254 Q3 snapshot actually shows.

**What exists:** 27 tracked facilities and projects in Kenya. 20 are operational. Only 8 of those publish a verified live IT load figure, which puts Kenya's measured, running compute base at 10.5 MW. That is a floor, not a ceiling, but it is the honest number.

**What is announced:** 230 MW of pipeline. 77 MW physically under construction (iXAfrica NBOX1.2, Africa Data Centres Nairobi 2, Nxtra at Tatu City), 53 MW committed (NBOX2 at Tilisi), and 100 MW resting on a single early-stage announcement, the Microsoft and G42 AI data centre.

**The geography tells its own story.** Nairobi holds 19 of the 27 records. Mombasa's 4 facilities are all operational. And every single non-operational project sits in the Nairobi metro.

**Connectivity is the mature layer.** Seven submarine cable systems are in service at Mombasa, from SEACOM and TEAMS in 2009 to 2Africa in 2024. Africa-1 has landed but is not counted live until readiness for service is declared.

Cables into the country are not the same thing as compute inside it.

Verified infrastructure is not announced infrastructure. DC254 tracks both, and never lets them blur.

## 3. FACEBOOK (simplified, conversational)

How much of Kenya's digital infrastructure is actually live?

We went through the whole DC254 dataset to answer that properly.

The short version: Kenya has 27 data centre facilities and projects tracked. 20 of them are real and running today. Everything else is under construction, committed, or still just announced.

The capacity story is even more interesting. Only 10.5 MW of IT load is verified as live. The announced pipeline is 230 MW. That gap surprises people, but it is normal: it is the difference between what exists today and what the market says may exist tomorrow. One project, the Microsoft and G42 AI data centre, accounts for 100 MW of that pipeline on its own.

The internet connections are the mature part. Seven undersea cable systems already reach Mombasa, and a new one (Africa-1) has landed but is not switched on for service yet. But note: cables landing at the coast are not the same as computing capacity in Nairobi.
