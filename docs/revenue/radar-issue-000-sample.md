## 1. Microsoft ADC Nairobi wind-down: REPORTED, no Microsoft statement yet

Multiple outlets (13-14 Oct) report that Microsoft is winding down its Africa
Development Centre in Nairobi, with staff informed on Monday 12 Oct and roughly
400 roles affected per staff reports. An on-record Microsoft response (13 Oct)
acknowledges "proposed changes affecting its engineering operations in Kenya"
and says Microsoft will continue operating in Kenya, but confirms neither
closure nor headcount. The CS for ICT met a Microsoft delegation on 12 Oct and
publicly pressed to retain talent.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU: hundreds of senior engineers and vendor-facing staff
enter the market. Integrators can hire engineers with hyperscaler-grade
experience that was previously out of reach. Recruiters: the pool is live now.

## 2. NIF: KSh 340 billion being deployed - CONFIRMED

Reuters (7 Oct): the National Infrastructure Fund is deploying KSh 340 billion
with bonds issued since July and full deployment targeted by June 2027, against
a KSh 3.6 trillion decade ambition. Digital infrastructure is named in the
CEO's eligible sector list. No data-centre-specific allocation is documented.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU: the buyer's funding environment is warming. Align vendor
proposals with NIF-eligible categories; we watch for the first DC allocation.

## 3. KDEAP fibre backbone tender: closes 10:00 EAT, 29 October 2026

The Kenya Digital Economy Acceleration Project (World Bank-funded, ICT
Authority-implemented) runs its subsea-protected national fibre backbone
procurement. The window closes on 29 October at 10:00 EAT (15 days from this
issue). Anyone bidding or sub-contracting needs local delivery partners now.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU: the week's most time-sensitive item. If you sell power,
cabinets, civils, splicing or last-mile to backbone projects, partner
shortlists are being finalised this week, not next month.

## 4. WIOCC: up to US$155 million DFC equity - CONFIRMED

Reuters (16 Sep): DFC's largest-ever equity investment, up to US$155 million in
WIOCC, earmarked to scale open-access digital infrastructure across its markets
including Kenya. Separately kept distinct: the US$300 million AFC and Vision
Invest facility announced in September.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU: carrier-backed builds get funded budgets. Transmission,
colocation and edge-site suppliers should be on WIOCC-group vendor radars now.
