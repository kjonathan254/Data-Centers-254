## The brief

**1. Microsoft winds down its Nairobi Africa Development Centre** — KENYA | 12–14 OCT | REPORTED
(MICROSOFT ACKNOWLEDGES "PROPOSED CHANGES"). Staff accounts (CIO Africa 12 Oct, tech-ish) say
employees were told Monday 12 Oct that the centre closes; ~400 roles at risk per staff reports
and media (WeeTracker "nearly 400"; Kenyans.co.ke 400–500) — a reported figure, not a Microsoft
number. Microsoft's on-record response (obtained by CIO Africa and tech-ish, carried by Business
Insider Africa 13 Oct) acknowledges "proposed changes affecting its engineering operations in
Kenya" and says it will continue operating in the country and supporting customers — it neither
confirms nor denies the closure and gives no headcount or dates. Status stays REPORTED, not
CONFIRMED. Context kept precise: the US$100M was the launch investment target (five years, up to
500 engineers across Nairobi + Lagos), not a spent sum; the ADC is a software-engineering hub —
a different asset from the stalled US$1B G42 campus, whose house record is unchanged; the ADC
second-site closure follows Lagos (May 2024). CS William Kabogo met a Microsoft delegation led
by Jeff Bullwinkel (VP, Deputy General Counsel) on 12 Oct and publicly stressed "retaining local
talent, protecting jobs at Microsoft's Nairobi engineering centre and sustaining investor
confidence". Same reporting names Oracle's September Nairobi cuts alongside fresh Kenyan job
adverts for DC technicians and AI infrastructure sales — a reallocation signal for the
workforce tracker.

**2. National Infrastructure Fund begins deploying KSh 340B** — KENYA | 7 OCT | CONFIRMED
(Reuters). Seed capital (≈US$2.6B) deploying initially via domestic government bonds; buying
began July; full deployment targeted by June 2027; ≈KSh 3.6T mobilisation ambition over a
decade. CEO James Mworia frames demand across energy, transport, logistics, water, agriculture
and digital infrastructure. No specific data-centre allocation identified — eligibility, not
allocation, is the story. Reporting test: do not imply DCs will receive funding until a
specific allocation or eligible project is documented.

**3. KDEAP fibre tender re-reported; 15 days to bid close** — KENYA | 13 OCT | HOUSE-COVERED.
W.Media's "Kenya commits US$100 million to 27,000 km" is the same KDEAP procurement the house
covered 13–14 Oct (KSh 13B attributed to the PS; 27,000 km announced target; framework tender
Lot 1 + Lot 2). US$100M is a currency equivalence, not a new commitment. Bids close 10:00 EAT
29 Oct 2026 — re-verify against ICTA records on publish day. Announced ≠ delivered labelling
mandatory on any re-post (article + explainer video both carry it).

**4. Standalone data-centre licence: still no finalisation** — KENYA | 8 SEP proposal | PROPOSED.
30-day window elapsed; 14 Oct follow-up found no adoption notice (Gazette 12181 covered other
categories, 14 Jul). The evidence that matters next: final framework, effective date,
transitional arrangements.

**5. CA opens Satellite Frequency Licensing Framework 2026 consultation** — KENYA | 13 OCT |
CONFIRMED. Draft framework published; comments due 6 Nov 2026 via prescribed template to
frequencyreturns@ca.go.ke (TechTrendsKE). Context: LEO broadband growth since Starlink's 2023
entry; MNO level-playing-field arguments. Second live CA consultation running concurrently.

**6. WIOCC financing: two distinct transactions** — PAN-AFRICAN | SEP, re-reported 9 OCT |
CONFIRMED. DFC equity of "up to US$155M" (Reuters 16 Sep; DFC's largest-ever equity); the 9 Oct
Reuters piece is commentary on DFC's Africa equity strategy. Separate: US$300M AFC + Vision
Invest agreement (WIOCC own site, early Sep; house-corroborated 10 Oct) targeting data centres,
terrestrial fibre, subsea. Do not aggregate; precision on "up to" and dates.

**7. ICASA signals broader regulatory approach** — SOUTH AFRICA | 13 OCT | REPORTED. ITWeb:
regulator expanding approach as satellite connectivity, cloud computing and AI reshape digital
infrastructure; satellite licensing framework consultation open. No specific new rules verified.
Useful Kenya benchmark — compare on actual text, not intent.

**8. PwC outlook: Kenya internet ad revenue to double by 2030** — AFRICA | CONFIRMED FORECAST.
US$313M (2025) → US$603M (2030), 14.0% CAGR, fastest in the outlook (trade coverage: "world's
fastest-growing internet ad market"); connectivity >75% of Kenyan household E&M spend.
Demand-side context for capacity theses; a forecast, not a measured outcome.
