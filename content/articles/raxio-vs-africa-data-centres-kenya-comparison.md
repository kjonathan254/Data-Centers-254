Two names keep coming up in the same breath for anyone shopping for data centre space in Kenya: Raxio and Africa Data Centres. Both are pan-African operators, both describe themselves in nearly identical language (carrier-neutral, Tier III, enterprise-grade), and both have Kenya in their growth plans. That is where the similarity ends, because one of them has doors open in Nairobi today and the other does not. This article separates the verifiable from the announced, in plain English, so you can make a decision on facts rather than press releases.

The need for this reality check is real. Comparison drafts circulating in the market have put these two operators side by side as if both were running Nairobi facilities, and even placed one of them at addresses it does not occupy. Before comparing anything, you verify. That is the house rule here, so that is what we did.

![Ribbon cutting at the NBO2 data centre launch](/images/nbo2-launch-ribbon-cutting.webp)

## First, the location claim, checked

One widely repeated draft comparison describes this matchup as "Mombasa Road versus Upper Hill or Karen". We fact-checked it, and it is wrong on the second half. Africa Data Centres' own website places its Nairobi flagship NBO1 at Sameer Business Park, Block A, on Mombasa Road (fetched 16 September 2026). PeeringDB, the industry's independent directory that operators themselves update, lists the same address (entry dated September 2025), and third-party facility trackers agree. No Africa Data Centres facility sits in Upper Hill or Karen.

Why does an address deserve a whole section? Because in this market, small factual drift spreads fast. A buyer who visits the wrong address, or prices one facility while believing it is another, is making a decision on fiction. The correct picture is simple: NBO1 sits on Mombasa Road, inside Sameer Business Park, and that is where the most densely connected building in Kenya stands, with four internet exchanges operating on site.

![The Nairobi Expressway stretching over Mombasa Road](/images/nairobi-expressway.webp)

## Africa Data Centres: what is verifiable

Africa Data Centres is the data centre arm of Cassava Technologies, a group headquartered in South Africa, and its Kenyan story is currently the more concrete one. The flagship, [NBO1](/directory/africa-dc-nairobi-1), is operational at Sameer Business Park as described above, with about 2,000 square metres of technical space listed in its network directory entry, and a reputation as the building where Kenyan networks meet: four internet exchanges under one roof, which is precisely what [carrier neutrality](/articles/what-is-colocation-kenya) is supposed to deliver.

Then there is the new one. In September 2026, Digital Realty and Africa Data Centres launched NBO2 in Nairobi, with about 6.4 megawatts of commissioned capacity reported by trade press (ET Datacenters, 10 September 2026; first reported 7 September 2026). We examined the launch and the "gateway" claims around it in a separate piece on [NBO2](/articles/digital-realty-nbo2-launch-nairobi). A megawatt, for scale, is roughly the draw of a few hundred Kenyan homes, so 6.4MW of commissioned data centre capacity is a serious expansion of what Nairobi can host.

The verifiable summary for ADC: one operational flagship with unmatched connectivity, one brand-new facility with a joint-venture partner, group ownership under a listed-technology family, and addresses you can physically visit tomorrow.

![Map showing data centre locations across Africa](/images/africa-dc-map.webp)

## Raxio: what is verifiable, and what is not

Raxio Group, established in 2018, presents itself as Africa's leading carrier-neutral Tier III operator, and its expansion record is genuine. Its Kampala facility opened in 2021 and its first Angola facility launched in late 2025 (Raxio pages and launch coverage). The money is real too: in July 2026 the group secured up to US$380 million to fund African expansion (Data Centre Magazine, 13 July 2026), and its earlier build-outs carried a US$12.24 million guarantee from MIGA, the World Bank Group's investment insurance arm, approved in September 2022. Institutional backing of that kind does not attach to paper companies; it means someone underwrote the build.

Kenya is squarely in the plan. Sector briefings, including an East African market assessment by the D4D Hub, describe Kenya as one of Raxio's target locations, with a first facility suggested to go live in 2026. And yet, as of 16 September 2026, no opened Nairobi facility is verifiable from primary sources: there is no launch announcement, no published address taking customers, no commissioning report. Our own directory, which holds every Kenyan facility to a verification bar of physical evidence and primary confirmation, lists Raxio Nairobi as early stage and unverified. That is not an accusation; proposals are how every operational facility began. It is a status label, and it should steer procurement decisions.

The distinction matters because these two operators are at different stages of the same journey. ADC's Kenya story has crossed the finish line of opening; Raxio's has not. Comparing them as equals today would be like comparing a delivered building to a rendering of the same building. Both exist, only one hosts servers.

## So who wins for a Kenyan buyer?

If the question is where to place hardware this quarter, the answer does not actually involve a versus: it involves the facilities that are open. Nairobi's operational, verifiable options include [NBO1](/directory/africa-dc-nairobi-1) on Mombasa Road, the new NBO2, iColo Nairobi One under Digital Realty, iXAfrica's NBOX1 campus, and PAIX, all covered with a checklist in our [Nairobi colocation buyer's guide](/articles/colocation-data-centres-nairobi-buyers-guide). Choosing among them is about power headroom, connectivity needs, price and support, not brand slogans.

Raxio's Kenya entry, when it lands, is good news even for people who will never buy from it. Nairobi's colocation market is thin on genuine carrier-neutral competition, and every new Tier III entrant tends to sharpen pricing and service across the market. The right posture is to watch, not wait: keep Raxio in the evaluation list for future tenders, and check our directory, which will move Raxio out of "unverified" the day the evidence exists.

One last discipline worth borrowing: whatever a sales deck says, ask for the three proofs that cannot be faked. An opening date you can verify, a Uptime Institute certificate (our [Tier ratings explainer](/articles/data-centre-tier-ratings-explained) shows what the labels mean), and the list of carriers physically on site. Operators with operating facilities produce all three without hesitation. That single habit will protect you from every marketing drift this fast-moving market will throw at you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Raxio open in Nairobi yet?

Not as of 16 September 2026. Raxio Group has publicly targeted Kenya as a key market, and sector briefings have suggested a first Kenyan facility going live in 2026, but no opened Nairobi facility is verifiable from primary sources: no launch announcement, address, or commissioning report has been published. DC254's directory therefore lists Raxio Nairobi as early stage and unverified, and we will update the moment that changes.

### Where is Africa Data Centres' NBO1 located?

NBO1 sits at Sameer Business Park, Block A, on Mombasa Road in Nairobi. That address is confirmed by Africa Data Centres' own website and by PeeringDB's independent listing (September 2025). It is the most densely connected data centre building in Kenya, with four internet exchanges on site, which means networks meet and exchange traffic inside the building.

### Who owns Africa Data Centres?

Africa Data Centres is part of Cassava Technologies, a technology group founded by Zimbabwean-born businessman Strive Masiyiwa and headquartered in South Africa. In Nairobi, the group also partnered with Digital Realty on the NBO2 facility, which launched in September 2026 with about 6.4MW of commissioned capacity reported by trade press.

### Who is behind Raxio Group?

Raxio Group, established in 2018, describes itself as Africa's leading carrier-neutral Tier III data centre operator, with facilities including its Kampala site opened in 2021 and a first Angola facility launched in late 2025. In July 2026 it secured up to US$380 million to fund African expansion, and its earlier build-outs drew a USD 12.24 million guarantee from MIGA, the World Bank Group's investment insurance arm (approved September 2022). The Kenya facility is announced but not yet open.

### Which should a Kenyan business choose today?

If you need rack space or compute capacity in Nairobi now, the operational options are Africa Data Centres NBO1, the new NBO2, iColo Nairobi One, iXAfrica NBOX1 and PAIX, all of which we cover in our colocation buyer's guide. Raxio is a name to watch: when it opens, carrier-neutral competition in Nairobi increases, which historically lowers prices for everyone. Until then, any decision involving Raxio should start with three questions: when does it open, is it Tier III certified, and which carriers are on site?
